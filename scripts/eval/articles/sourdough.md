# What lives in a starter

A sourdough starter is flour and water that has been left to ferment. It looks like paste, but it is a small ecosystem: wild yeasts and lactic acid bacteria that arrived on the flour, on your hands and in the air. Feeding the starter with fresh flour and water every day or two keeps those microbes growing and pushes out the ones that cannot cope.

The two groups do different jobs. The yeasts eat sugars from the flour and release carbon dioxide, which is what makes bread rise. The bacteria also eat sugars, but they mostly produce lactic and acetic acids. Those acids give sourdough its tang and make the starter too acidic for many spoilage microbes.

# Why the timing matters

A starter rises after a feeding, peaks, and then falls as the food runs out. Bakers try to use it near the peak, when the yeast population is most active. A starter used long after its peak is still alive but sluggish, and the bread rises slowly.

Temperature changes the balance. A warm kitchen speeds everything up, so a starter peaks sooner and turns sour faster. A cool kitchen slows it down, which some bakers use on purpose: a starter kept in the fridge needs feeding only once a week.

# From starter to loaf

Only a small amount of starter goes into a loaf, often a fifth of the flour weight or less. The rest of the dough is fresh flour, water and salt. Over several hours the starter's microbes spread through the dough, and the long, slow rise lets enzymes and acids change the flavour and texture of the crumb.

Lots of people assume the sour taste comes from the yeast. Most of it comes from the bacteria. A baker who wants a milder loaf can use a young starter, a warmer rise or a shorter fermentation, all of which give the acids less time to build up.

# Keeping it alive

A neglected starter usually is not dead. A layer of grey liquid on top is alcohol the yeast produced while hungry; pour it off, feed the starter, and it often recovers within a few feedings. Mould with fuzzy colours is different, and the safe choice then is to start again.
