# Color theory (sampler)

Two squares, the same grey. You'll swear they're different. In this sampler you find out why color
lies and how to make it sing: you drag two greys together until the illusion breaks, turn orange
into brown with one dial, mix spotlights and inks, rank colors by how light they really look, and
spin a hue wheel until orange glows.

**Your guide:** Ochre, a French chameleon painter whose skin shifts with its mood. Flamboyant,
opinionated, secretly very precise. "Hue is the gossip. Value does all the work."
**World:** The Prism Studio, a sunlit painter's loft drawn as a sketchbook. Stops are paint jars;
the terminus is *Rescue the Poster*.

## What you'll do

| Stop | You'll be able to |
|---|---|
| Colors lie: simultaneous contrast | Predict how a surround shifts a color's lightness and tint |
| Hue, saturation, lightness | Describe and set any color with three dials, and tell saturation from lightness |
| Mixing light vs mixing paint | Predict what lights and inks make when they overlap |
| Value does the heavy lifting | Rank colors by perceived value and use the squint test |
| The color wheel and complements | Find a complement and use it to make a color pop or calm down |
| **Rescue the Poster** (terminus) | Fix a poster's values, accent, a contested grey and its output files |

Beyond the terminus the rest of the map is drawn in as planned stops: color as light and the
cones, harmonies, warm and cool, hex and RGB in code, contrast ratios and color blindness, color
constancy (the dress), palettes, color meaning and its myths, and two big bosses (Fix the Ugly
Poster, An accessible brand palette). Each one is an open invitation to help write it.

## Accessibility

Every color is named in words (and hex where it matters) as well as shown, so no answer depends on
seeing a hue. The six interactives in `assets/interactive/` use real buttons and sliders with
keyboard access and live captions that name the result (for example "where the lit beams overlap:
cyan").

## Honesty notes

- Perceived lightness values (yellow about 97, blue about 32) are CIELAB L* for the pure sRGB
  colors. The 21/72/7 weights are the WCAG relative-luminance coefficients, rounded.
- "Around 1 in 12 men" with red-green color vision deficiency is the NHS figure for the UK; it
  varies between populations.
- The ink mixer uses ideal inks. Real pigments mix less cleanly, which is why printers add black.
- Screen complements use the RGB/HSL wheel; the painter's red-yellow-blue wheel is named whenever
  it is used.
