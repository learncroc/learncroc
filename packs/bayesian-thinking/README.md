# Bayesian thinking (sampler)

A test catches 90% of cases and says you're positive. You're probably fine. By the end of this
sampler that will feel obvious, because you'll have counted it yourself in a crowd of 1,000 people.

**Your guide:** Inspector Prior, a night-shift detective who never says "certain".
**World:** The Detective's Corkboard. Stops are case cards; the terminus is *The Positive Test*.

## What you'll do

| Stop | You'll be able to |
|---|---|
| Start with how common | Ask for the base rate before trusting a clue (Steve: librarian or farmer?) |
| Out of how many? | Turn percentages into people, and relative risks into counts |
| Flip the question | Tell "of the sick" from "of the positives" |
| Bayes with 1,000 people | Work out what a positive test really means |
| Today's answer, tomorrow's prior | Update again when a second clue arrives |
| **The Positive Test** (terminus) | Walk a friend through her screening result, step by step |

Beyond the terminus the rest of the map is drawn in as planned stops (likelihood ratios, odds,
calibration, Monty Hall, randomness, regression to the mean, expected value and *The Cold Case*).
Each one is an open invitation to help write it.

## Honesty notes

- Every case is fictional. Numbers are illustrative unless a source is named on the stop.
- The classic screening numbers (1%, 90%, 9%) and the doctors' answers come from Gigerenzer et al.
  (2007). The Steve description is quoted from Tversky & Kahneman (1974).
- This pack teaches probability. It is not medical advice: real results belong with a clinician.
